# CREATIVE DIRECTION AND ART DIRECTION

## Concept
A quiet, premium digital heritage exhibition. The visitor enters a dim cave gallery, learns what Ajanta is, then scrolls through a five-panel retelling. Treat paintings like museum objects: generous breathing room, respectful crops, legible labels, and historically accurate captions. The visual experience must feel tactile and crafted, not like a modern dashboard or a children's clip-art site.

## Visual principles
- **Artwork first:** images command attention; text never obscures a figure or important painting detail.
- **Authentic vs interpreted:** authentic mural photographs are labelled as historical works; original narrative art is clearly identified as contemporary interpretation.
- **Materiality:** dark basalt/umber, muted pigments, faint plaster grain, restrained brass/ochre rules.
- **Rhythm:** immersive hero, concise context, large story panels, reflection, sources and team credits.
- **Restraint:** minimal chrome, no overuse of glow, blur, floating particles, thick borders or pill cards.

## Imagery hierarchy
1. Hero: verified Ajanta cave/mural photograph as editorial hero, or labelled original illustration fallback.
2. Ajanta context: verified site/interior image and a verified surviving mural image.
3. Story: use an authenticated *matching* historical scene only when confidently identified; otherwise original hand-painted mural-inspired panels.
4. Sources: thumbnail credits with artwork title, creator/photographer, institution, licence and source link.

## Scene illustration style
Commission/create five cohesive compositions with hand-painted contours, stylised figures, detailed elephant anatomy, layered forest plants, expressive body language, weathered pigment variation and culturally informed architecture. Use a fixed character model for the white elephant and two blind parents; retain visual continuity of tree and path. Avoid cute simplified circles, clip-art silhouettes, 3D renders and generic fantasy art. Do not add captions inside raster art.

## Exact panel briefs
1. Forest caregiving: adult white elephant offering foliage to two older elephants; deep forest, repeating bent tree, intimate composition.
2. Lost traveller: traveller near winding path, elephant guiding him toward clearing; compassion without caricature.
3. Betrayal: man revealing forest location to royal attendants; elephant being led away; tense framing, no graphic violence.
4. Royal courtyard: untouched food foreground, quiet elephant, king listening; measured architectural detail.
5. Reunion: same bent tree as scene 1, elephant returning to parents; open composition and restored warmth.

## Cropping
Use `object-fit: contain` or deliberate aspect-specific layouts for historically important paintings. Never crop away heads, faces, or the narrative action. `object-position` is configured per asset. Include a view-full-image link when appropriate.
