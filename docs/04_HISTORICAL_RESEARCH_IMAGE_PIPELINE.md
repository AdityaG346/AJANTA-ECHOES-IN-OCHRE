## Exact research workflow
1. Search by cave, subject and Jataka name; use Google Images only to discover candidates, not as a licence source.
2. Prefer UNESCO, archaeological institutions, museums, IGNCA and Wikimedia Commons pages with clear file-level provenance.
3. Open each candidate file page. Record title, cave/location, subject, creator/photographer, source URL, rights/licence and modification requirements.
4. Distinguish authentic Ajanta artwork from copies, modern restorations, reproductions and other sites.
5. Confirm whether the image illustrates the Matri-Poshaka narrative. If not, use only as general context with truthful caption.
6. Download permitted source image; keep asset locally, create web-optimised WebP/JPEG, and preserve source metadata in credits.
7. Use alt text describing what is actually visible, not an invented scene.
8. Do not crop away key figures; make a full-image option if needed.
9. Never present modern-generated art as a surviving mural.

## Minimum final visual inventory
- Hero: one verified Ajanta mural or cave photo, or an explicit original-art fallback.
- About: one site/interior photo and one genuine mural detail, both credited.
- Story: five visually cohesive original mural-inspired panels unless exact corresponding historical paintings are verified and reuse permitted.
- Texture: self-created subtle plaster texture or properly licensed local texture.
- Icons: local or inline SVG, licence acknowledged if required.

## Legal/ethical constraints
A photo of an ancient painting may have independent rights. Never assume “old painting = free photo”. Do not scrape a search thumbnail or bypass restrictions. Do not hotlink arbitrary sites. Do not invent a licence. If rights cannot be confirmed, use a local original artwork fallback and record the unresolved image.

## Historical quality gate
Do not use unrelated Chaddanta/Vessantara paintings to imply a Matri-Poshaka episode. It is acceptable to show them as clearly labelled *other examples of Ajanta narrative painting*, but keep the story panels identified as modern interpretation unless authenticated.
